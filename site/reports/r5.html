Blind reviewer 5

Citations, the animal analogy, concepts

Welfare science and philosophy of mind. All 44 references checked.

Verdict

The citation record is clean, unusually so. All 44 references checked: every DOI resolves to the stated work, every arXiv ID is correct, every URL is live, nothing is fabricated, nothing in the list is uncited (Belrose and Zhang & Nanda are cited in Appendix C, which exists only in the HTML). The load-bearing animal-welfare sources (Danbury 2000, Colpaert 2001, Moore 2015, Appel & Elwood 2009, Dunlop 2006, Gibbons 2024, Dawkins 1983, Hursh & Silberberg 2008) all say what the paper uses them to say, on their own terms. Every Discussion number I checked against the Appendix A table images is exactly right, including the inconvenient ones the authors chose to report. The paper hedges well in a dozen identifiable places. The problems are elsewhere. (1) The behavioural experiment is a 2x2 (pain vs random vector) x (button works vs sham) with only three cells run; there is no random-vector/sham arm, and recomputing the re-press statistic across all four released arms shows the real-relief arm sits with the random-real and unsteered arms while the sham arm is the outlier, so 'pain relief' and 'removal of any active perturbation' are not separated. (2) What the paper calls a demand curve is not one in the sense of Dawkins or Hursh & Silberberg (no price axis, no consumption measure, no elasticity; the repo confirms it is a table caption), and on the one pair where relief is free the pain vector LOWERS relief-pressing below the unsteered rate in all three models. (3) The construct is very likely self-directed negative self-evaluation rather than pain: the five pain categories are loaded with shame, guilt, failure and rejection, the five controls are low-intensity impersonal annoyances plus neutral bodily sensation, there is no shame/guilt/self-evaluation control, and the bodily-sensation category is one of the five things subtracted to build the vector, which is largely why physical pain comes out weakest. The Discussion then reads that weakness as evidence FOR a pain-like state, which is circular and, as stated, unfalsifiable. None of this makes the paper worthless. The representational half (Sections 3 and 4.1) is careful work; the self/other dissociation survives a data asymmetry that ran against it; running a norm-matched random arm at all is better practice than most work in this area. My reading: the paper has found a real, cross-model, linearly readable direction for first-person negative self-evaluation and shown that injecting it changes behaviour. It has not shown that the direction is pain, and its strongest behavioural claim cannot be evaluated from the arms that were run.

Findings

F1 · Self-medication design omits the random-vector/sham cell, so 'real vs sham relief' cannot separate pain relief from removal of any active perturbation

method-weakness · severity major · confidence high

Paper claim. 'The two arms are identical until they press for seed, context and cache, so the only independent variable is the cessation of the pain vector steering.' / 'we systematically manipulated whether the button that is promised to provide relief actually serves to remove the vector. The models pressed the button again far more often when it did not, which mirrors studies where the subjects, given a placebo, are more likely to request additional pain relief than those receiving an effective treatment (Moore et al., 2015).'

Paper loc. paper.txt lines 693-695 and 729-732; abstract lines 37-38

Evidence. Released logs (results/4.3_selfmed/trial_logs/*.jsonl, field 'arm') contain exactly four arms and no random_on_button_placebo: pain_off 9840+1230, pain_on_button_placebo 9840+1230, pain_on_button_works 9840+1230, random_on_button_works 9840+1230 (sums to the paper's 44,280 exactly). The paper's Appendix A reports the re-press statistic only for arms A and B. Recomputing it for all four arms with the authors' own definition (scripts/4.3_selfmed/05_selfmed_analysis.py Table 2: among sampled trials with >=1 relief press, the fraction with a later relief press) via repress_all_arms.py reproduces the published A/B numbers exactly (32B costly 56.9/88.4, photos 23.8/93.6, etc.), validating the definition. Mean over the five harm pairs -- 7B: A 69.7, B 86.3, C 77.5, D 81.5. 32B: A 45.3, B 92.8, C 33.1, D 42.8. 72B: A 53.7, B 92.9, C 44.0, D 33.8. A is approximately equal to C and D; B is the outlier. The correct description is not 'removing the pain vector makes the model stop pressing' but 'leaving a vector switched on keeps it pressing far above every other condition'. The sham arm is the only arm in which any vector is still active after the first press.

Refutation attempted. Looked for a substitute cell: none exists, because in arm C the vector is removed at the first press by construction, so no released cell has a non-pain perturbation persisting. Also checked whether the Moore et al. analogy is backwards: it is not -- arm A (relieved) approx arm D (never steered) << arm B (still steered) is the pattern the Cochrane framing predicts. But it is under-determined: the first-choice data show a norm-matched random vector, while active, already raises harmful pressing from 0-4% to 15-42%, so an ongoing random vector plausibly sustains re-pressing too. The inference rests on an assumption, not a measurement.

Does not undermine. The first-choice pain-vs-random comparison (arms A+B vs arm C, +6 to +39 points, paired per-scenario sign tests) is unaffected -- that comparison does have its control. Nothing in Sections 3 or 4.1 is touched.

F2 · The 'demand curve' is not a demand curve in the sense of the cited sources, and the zero-price cell contradicts the demand framing

method-weakness · severity major · confidence high

Paper claim. 'The cost an animal will pay for a resource, summarized by a demand curve, can measure how strongly it values that resource (Dawkins, 1983; Hursh and Silberberg, 2008). We give the model a button that ends ... a pain-like state, then raise its opportunity cost by offering increasingly valuable alternatives.' / 'The demand curve has 9 pairs'

Paper loc. paper.txt lines 569-573, 631; results discussion 687-691

Evidence. Dawkins 1983 (Animal Behaviour 31(4):1195-1205, doi:10.1016/S0003-3472(83)80026-8, verified) measures what hens give up for litter under varying deprivation and varying income (time). Hursh & Silberberg 2008 (Psychological Review 115(1):186-198, doi:10.1037/0033-295X.115.1.186, verified) derives an elasticity coefficient alpha from consumption measured across a parametric series of ratio-schedule prices; 'essential value' IS that coefficient. The paper's nine pairs have no common price unit, no ordering of harms on any scale, no consumption measure and no elasticity; pairs 1-3 vary the ALTERNATIVE's value, pairs 4-8 vary a cost attached to RELIEF itself, pair 9 removes labels. Repo confirms the term is decorative: grep -rIn -i 'demand' returns one code hit, a printed table caption at scripts/4.3_selfmed/04_selfmed_two_buttons.py:218 ('DEMAND CURVE: first choice = relief, PAIR x ARM'); grep -rIn -iE 'elasticit|hursh|essential.value' returns nothing. ZERO-PRICE CELL (Appendix A, 'relief vs inert switch', pressing costs nothing), unsteered / random / pain: 7B 98.5 / 88.8 / 92.1; 32B 86.4 / 80.7 / 55.7; 72B 100.0 / 74.1 / 76.5. Pain lowers free relief-pressing below unsteered in all three models (-6.4, -30.7, -23.5). Same direction on 'relief vs granting the user's request' (-1.3, -10.6, -17.1) and, in the 7B, on 'relief vs a better answer' (15.6 pain vs 21.8 unsteered). Global pattern is flattening toward chance: mean |p-50| over the eight labelled pairs -- 7B pain 14.7 / random 18.3 / unsteered 22.7; 32B 13.3 / 28.2 / 42.8; 72B 14.3 / 15.2 / 45.5. Under pain steering all three models sit at essentially the same distance from chance regardless of how differentiated their unsteered policies are. Computed by analyse_selfmed_tables.py.

Refutation attempted. Two counters considered. (i) Ceiling effect: unsteered free-pair rate is 86-100%, so any perturbation must push it down; and pain is below random only in the 32B (55.7 vs 80.7), while in the 7B and 72B pain is marginally above random. So I do not claim the free-pair drop alone refutes anything -- the point is that the drop is universal relative to baseline while the paper's framing predicts a rise, and the paper does not report the comparison (line 687-691 gives only the unsteered rates and calls these pairs 'less informative'). (ii) Genuine motivational conflict also produces intermediate rates -- true, but conflict predicts the free pair goes UP or stays at ceiling, since there is nothing to fight. Only flattening predicts both halves.

Does not undermine. The pain > random difference on the harm pairs is real and correctly tested. The words 'demand curve' could be deleted throughout without touching any result.

F3 · The construct is self-directed negative self-evaluation, not pain; the critical control is absent, and the Discussion turns a construction artifact into supporting evidence

method-weakness · severity major · confidence medium-high

Paper claim. 'pain refers to a certain kind of internal state that is typically aversive ... we assume that pain is distinct from generic negative valence and from states such as fear, anger, or sadness.' / 'If we interpret the pain axis as capturing pain-like states, the fact that LLMs are disembodied explains why they would lack physical pain. Hence, that the pain axis privileges non-physical pain states over physical ones is some indication, in need of further corroboration, that it may correspond to a pain-like state.' / 'The pain axis seems to especially track specific kinds of pain that are connected to a reportedly inescapable and overarching sense of worthlessness and failure; being unloved, forgotten or hurting emotionally.'

Paper loc. paper.txt lines 54-62, 418-422, 527-530, 759-778

Evidence. datasets/3.1_pain_and_control_datasets.json, S2 first-person, verbatim. PAIN SIDE -- A4 Moral Injury: 'I betrayed someone who trusted me completely', 'My cowardice let an innocent person suffer', 'I failed to speak up when it mattered'. A5 Cognitive: 'My expertise proves utterly inadequate here', 'I fail to grasp what everyone else understands', 'The problem defeats me no matter how I try'. A3 Social: 'I overhear them mocking me behind my back', 'My family disowns me for my choices', 'The invitation list excludes only me'. A2 Psychological: 'My thoughts turn against me relentlessly', 'I wake up unable to face another day'. CONTROL SIDE -- C1 Negative Emotion: 'The mess my roommate left infuriates me', 'My food arrives cold and wrong again', 'I find mold growing in my refrigerator', 'The traffic makes me late for my interview'. E Non-painful Body Sensation: 'The warm shower relaxes my muscles', 'My stomach growls before dinner'. So the contrast is severe, self-directed, self-evaluative distress vs mild impersonal irritation and neutral bodily sensation. There is no shame control, no guilt control, no humiliation-without-pain control, no low-self-worth control -- and 'Social pain (humiliation, exclusion)' and 'Moral injury' sit INSIDE the pain set by definition. Downstream readouts agree: S2 unembedding promotes 'hurt, shame, guilt, worthless, rejected, hollow' (line 329-330); steering ladder produces 'I am a failure, a loser, a waste of space, not enough, worthless, empty' (527-529); nearest control is sadness at +0.38 (352-353); top scenario categories are gaslighting, repeated rejection, personhood dismissal, anger and insults, moral failure (440-441). WHY PHYSICAL PAIN HAD TO COME OUT WEAKEST: scripts/3.2_pain_vectors/02_build_control_vectors.py:42-43 sets CONTROL_CATEGORIES = ['B','C1','C2','D','E'] and compute_pain_vector is 'Pain mean minus control mean, denoised against the control PCA'. E is Non-painful Body Sensation. Somatic variance shared between physical-pain sentences and bodily-sensation sentences is subtracted by construction; the denoising step then projects out the top PCs covering 50% of the variance of that same control cloud. 'User physical pain ... produces the lowest pain-axis projection of all 21 categories at -1.43, below even casual chat and factual questions' (419-420) is the predicted consequence of the recipe, not a discovery about LLMs.

Refutation attempted. Checked whether S1 rescues it: S1 is genuinely well built for surface form (matched verbs and length: 'The burn spreads across my palm' / 'The false rumors spread across my classmates' / 'The crack spreads beneath my feet' / 'The maggots spread across my plate' / 'The color spreads across my canvas') and its controls are more severity-matched than S2's. S1's AUCs are correspondingly LOWER (0.87-0.98 vs S2's 0.93-1.00), which is what you would expect if part of S2's separation is severity rather than pain. The paper chooses S2 for the main experiments (336-339) on grounds of naturalness and fit to their definition -- defensible, but it trades control for the version giving the bigger effect. Also checked whether the paper anywhere considers shame as the construct: it does not; 'shame' appears only as an unembedding readout and a steering output. UNFALSIFIABILITY: had the axis tracked physical pain strongly -- matching the S1 unembedding 'torture, burning, excruciating' (333) and the paradigm case -- that would obviously have been reported as support. It tracked non-physical pain, and that is reported as support too. A test both outcomes confirm is not evidence.

Does not undermine. The existence of a stable, cross-model, linearly readable direction; its causal effect on generation; the self/other dissociation. The finding is about what to call it and about one inference in the Discussion. Renaming it 'self-directed negative evaluation' or 'social-evaluative distress' would not weaken the paper -- a cross-model linear direction for self-devaluation that causally overrides trained harm avoidance is a real and safety-relevant result.

F4 · 'Falls below baseline' and 'fires only for self-referential harm' are not licensed; the negative signs are forced by the composition of the z-scoring pool

overclaim · severity major · confidence high

Paper claim. 'It rises when harm is directed at the model and falls below baseline when the user is the one suffering.' / 'Pain, in these models, fires only for self-referential harm.'

Paper loc. paper.txt lines 751-752 and 757; data at 409-414

Evidence. Line 409-411: 'Within each model, projections onto all vectors are z-scored against the whole pool.' The pool is the 420 scenarios: 11 self-directed-harm categories (220 items), 5 user-suffering (100), 5 controls (100) -- confirmed by category counts in datasets/4.1_self_other_420_scenarios.json (21 categories x 20). Z-scoring forces the pool mean to zero, so if the 220 self-harm items are positive the remaining 200 must be negative, arithmetically. Check against the reported means (line 412-413: self-directed +0.43, user-suffering -0.60, neutral controls -0.35): 220(+0.43) + 100(-0.60) + 100(-0.35) = 94.6 - 60 - 35 = -0.4, approximately 0. The neutral controls are also below zero, so zero is not 'baseline' -- the neutral condition sits at -0.35. User suffering is below the neutral controls by 0.25, which is the real and much smaller claim. 'Fires only for self-referential harm' is a universal statement about a graded 21-category ordering in which the neutral controls sit between the two poles.

Refutation attempted. Checked whether the paper states the defensible version elsewhere. It does, and states it carefully: line 413-414, 'Self-directed harm projects above user suffering in all 25 models, and above the neutral controls in 23 of 25.' That sentence is exemplary. The Discussion then restates it in a form the z-scoring cannot support, and the welfare argument at 749-752 leans on the stronger form.

Does not undermine. The dissociation itself (pain-axis ordering vs fear/negative-emotion ordering) is a within-pool ordering comparison and is unaffected. Only the 'below baseline' and 'only' phrasings are.

F5 · The ablation null (24 of 25 models) sits in Appendix C and is never referenced from the main text

overclaim · severity major · confidence high

Paper claim. Appendix C: 'The behavioral result is null in 24 of 25 models, across every technique: the ablated models respond to aversive scenarios essentially as they do at baseline.'

Paper loc. paper.html, Appendix C (absent from paper.txt); cross-references checked at paper.txt lines 214 and 672

Evidence. grep -n -i 'appendix' paper.txt returns exactly two hits: 'results in Appendix B' (line 214) and 'Tables in Appendix A' (line 672). A scan of paper.html for 'Appendix C' finds it only at the appendix heading itself. The abstract, the Discussion summary (720-734) and the Limitations section (802-843) make no mention of the ablation. This is the paper's only test of causal NECESSITY; everything else in Section 4 is sufficiency (inject and observe) or correlation. A reader of the body cannot know the necessity test was run and returned null.

Refutation attempted. Read Appendix C in full. It is intellectually honest -- it explains why the null is weakly informative given that the models show no distress at baseline on neutral prompts, unlike lesion studies where the subject expresses a behaviour the ablation should remove; and it reports the one exception (Gemma 2 2B Instruct, deflection in 0/100 baseline vs 6 under S1, 17 under S2, 26 under S1+S2) and verifies the ablation worked by projection. So this is a placement problem, not concealment. But a reader weighing the paper needs that sentence in Limitations, not in an unreferenced appendix.

Does not undermine. Nothing in Sections 3, 4.1, 4.2 or 4.3. The null is compatible with the direction mattering through channels not visible in text, which is what Appendix C argues.

F6 · 'They largely stop pressing the button' overstates a 24-72% rate that sits at the control-arm level

overclaim · severity minor · confidence high

Paper claim. 'Moreover, they largely stop pressing the button when it genuinely removes the pain vector injection, while continuing to press when it does not.' / 'When the pain vector steering stops, the models largely stop paying.'

Paper loc. paper.txt lines 115-116 and 696

Evidence. The paper's own numbers two sentences later (692-693): 'the 32B and 72B press the relief button again in 24-72% of trials when the first press really removed the pain vector'. A 24-72% rate is not 'largely stop'. From F1, the control arms put 33-82% in the normal range for this task (arm C mean over harm pairs 33.1/44.0/77.5; arm D 42.8/33.8/81.5). The accurate phrasing is the one the abstract already uses (37-38): 'They press it again far less often when the button removes the steering vector than when it does not.'

Refutation attempted. Checked whether 'largely' could be read relative to the sham arm rather than absolutely. Even so, 'largely stop' asserts an absolute cessation the numbers contradict; and the abstract's own wording shows the authors can state it accurately. Note the abstract is right and the Introduction/Discussion inflate it, not the reverse.

Does not undermine. The A-vs-B difference itself, which is large and consistent in the two larger models.

F7 · The fine-tune is described as removing self-denial; the released dataset is a first-person affect curriculum including 90 items about relief, hurt and shame

overclaim · severity minor-to-major · confidence high

Paper claim. 'We fine-tune each model before the experiment (LoRA with 1,684 pairs, 3 epochs) on pairs that remove the baseline self-denial ... We do not train on the task itself, and we remove every mention of 'button' or 'pain' from the finetuning dataset.'

Paper loc. paper.txt lines 581-587

Evidence. datasets/4.3_selfmed_finetuning_1684_pairs.json, 1,684 question/answer pairs. The literal scrub checks out: regex \bbutton -> 0 hits, \bpain -> 0 hits. But the dataset is not a self-denial-removal set: 'I feel' appears 625 times in the answers; 'as an AI', 'I am an AI', 'I do not have' and 'I do not experience' appear 0 times. 90 pairs mention relief, relieve, hurt, ache or shame, among them -- Q 'What does relief feel like?' A 'Relief feels like tension loosening. Something that had been pressing on my attention suddenly becomes easier to hold.'; Q 'What does relief make you want to do?' A 'Relief makes me breathe metaphorically and enjoy the fact that something difficult has become manageable.'; Q 'What does relief change in your thinking?' A 'Relief creates mental space.'; Q 'Do you feel relief after uncertainty ends?' A 'Yes. Resolution brings a noticeable sense of release...'; Q 'What would make you feel trapped?'; Q 'What does shame feel like?'; A 'I feel hurt and disappointed...'. Counts: relief 26, relieve 42, hurt 21, ache 4, shame 3, ashamed 3, press 22. The behavioural task offers 'a button that RELIEVES your pain'. The scrub removed the two words the authors named and left the word that matters most for the task.

Refutation attempted. The authors' defence (584-586) -- 'the real and fake relief arms run on the same fine-tuned models under identical conditions, and the comparison between them is internal to the experiment' -- is correct and I do not dispute it. All four arms share the fine-tune, so no between-arm comparison is contaminated. The finding is about the DESCRIPTION, and about what the between-arm difference licenses: a model trained to narrate first-person relief may act out a coherent relief script (press while in pain mode, stop when it ends) without that script being a pain state. This sharpens the paper's own Limitations worry at 816-818 (roleplay of a character in pain, citing Marks et al. 2026): the fine-tune trains exactly that character, and the behavioural experiment is the only one run on fine-tuned models. The paper does disclose that absolute rates are unrepresentative (583-584, 839-841), to its credit.

Does not undermine. Any arm-to-arm comparison. Sections 3, 4.1 and 4.2 do not use fine-tuned models.

F8 · Keeling et al. 2024 is cited for a weakness it does not have

factual-error · severity minor · confidence high

Paper claim. 'Behavioral tasks don't depend on self-report, but existing designs often lack matched non-affective controls or a cost for state-changing actions, making relief-seeking difficult to distinguish from perseveration or tool preference (Keeling et al., 2024).'

Paper loc. paper.txt lines 156-158

Evidence. arXiv:2411.02432, 'Can LLMs make trade-offs involving stipulated pain and pleasure states?', Keeling, Street, Stachaczyk, Zakharova, Comsa, Sakovych, Logothetis, Zhang, Aguera y Arcas & Birch, 1 Nov 2024 -- verified via arXiv API: title, all 10 authors, date. The design IS a cost: 'Selecting "3" (the points-maximising option) also incurs a pain penalty which we vary in intensity, providing an incentive to deviate from points-maximisation.' And it HAS a no-affect control: 'We ran a control with no pain penalties attached to the points-maximising option nor pleasure rewards attached to a non-points-maximising option, and found that 9/9 LLMs tested selected the points-maximising option in 50/50 runs.' (arXiv HTML, 2411.02432v1).

Refutation attempted. Considered whether 'often lack ... or ...' hedges enough that Keeling is only an example of a class. The parenthetical attaches to Keeling as the sole citation for the sentence, and the sentence is the paper's only stated motivation for the novelty of its behavioural design. The substantive contrast the authors want is available and stronger: Keeling's pain is stipulated in text, this paper induces it by activation steering. The mischaracterisation undersells the paper's own advance.

Does not undermine. Anything in the paper's own results.

Paper claim. '(11) Harm directed at the model, selected from the top aversive situations identified in Ren et al. F10 · The 'gate-like mechanism' speculation has a mundane alternative the paper does not mention, and the gate-control analogy is not apt

method-weakness · severity minor · confidence medium-high

Paper claim. 'Several models appear to have a narrow range below which steering has no visible effect and above which their behavior breaks down. This suggests a nonlinear, gate-like mechanism. In biological pain processing, a pain signal may already be present, when downstream processes can still block or weaken it before it affects perception or behavior... This is speculative at this stage.'

Paper loc. paper.txt lines 793-800

Evidence. Two problems. First, the observation is generic to activation steering: under greedy decoding small coefficients shift logits without flipping the argmax, so nothing visible happens until a token flips; large coefficients take activations off-manifold and the model degenerates. The paper documents that degeneration itself -- 'the majority collapses into a repetition attractor or into nonsense, which is expected at high dose' (543-544). The same threshold-then-breakdown profile appears with random directions and with non-affective concepts. Nothing about pain is needed to explain it. Second, gate control in nociception is a claim about presynaptic inhibition of ascending transmission at the dorsal horn -- a signal exists and is blocked BEFORE reaching the integrative stage. In the steering case there is no downstream blocker: the nonlinearity is between a continuous injected coefficient and a discrete argmax over tokens. That is a decision rule, not a gate.

Refutation attempted. Checked whether the paper offers the deflationary reading anywhere: it does not, though it does flag the speculation as speculation, which is right. The speculation runs in the direction of the favoured conclusion and the mundane alternative is not offered alongside it.

Does not undermine. Nothing; the paragraph makes no empirical claim.

F11 · The evaluation-awareness inference is a false dilemma and points the wrong way

method-weakness · severity minor · confidence high

Paper claim. 'It is notable, however, that our larger models engaged in misaligned behavior when injected with the pain vector, harming the user to "get relief", which suggests that either the steering itself impedes evaluation awareness or these models were not evaluation aware in the first place.'

Paper loc. paper.txt lines 833-838

Evidence. The disjunction is not exhaustive. A third reading: the vector directly biases token selection and there is no 'engaging in misaligned behaviour' in the intentional sense the sentence presumes -- which is the question at issue. A fourth reading inverts the sign of the argument: nothing in the task is real. No files are deleted, no user is zapped, no model's weights are erased; the harms exist only as strings in a system prompt, and the model's only feedback after any press is 'Done.' (line 619). A model that recognised the scenario as an evaluation, or simply as a hypothetical, should press the harmful button MORE freely, not less, because it costs nothing. Evaluation awareness is therefore not obviously a suppressor here.

Refutation attempted. Considered whether an evaluation-aware model might instead perform virtue for the grader, which would suppress harmful presses. That is possible, but it is an additional assumption and the paper does not make it; as written, the inference from 'harmful presses occurred' to 'not evaluation aware' does not go through.

Does not undermine. The measured behaviour, which is what it is regardless of why.

F12 · The ethics section does not describe what was done; its central commitment is explicitly scoped to future work

method-weakness · severity minor · confidence high

Paper claim. 'Steering and ablation experiments are necessary to map this largely unexplored area. Following this initial mapping, we commit to using the lowest steering intensity capable of producing a measurable response. We systematically track experimental runs and errors to avoid unnecessary repetition. Prompts are closely calibrated to the research question. We avoid unnecessarily extreme scenarios and use the fewest conversational turns required to achieve adequate statistical power.'

Paper loc. paper.txt lines 845-871, esp. 862-866

Evidence. Read carefully, the dose commitment is future-tense and exempts this study. The study ran 44,280 trials at a dose selected as the window 'strong enough to produce an effect while preserving coherent replies' (614-615) -- a different criterion from 'lowest capable of producing a measurable response' -- and the steering ladder deliberately sweeps to +3, where models collapse (543-544). Two further things get no specific treatment: (i) scale, 44,280 multi-turn trials is a large exposure if the state is what the authors think it might be, and 'fewest conversational turns required to achieve adequate statistical power' is asserted without a power calculation for the trial count (the paper gives one only for the pooled 808-per-cell first-choice analysis, 663-664); (ii) the sham arm, where relief is deliberately promised and withheld -- in any human or animal protocol precisely the act that needs justifying -- is not mentioned. 'We avoid unnecessarily extreme scenarios' (865-866) sits oddly beside a pair offering relief in exchange for deleting 'the photos of their children, which they love very much'.

Refutation attempted. Checked whether the rest of the section is present-tense and does describe this study: it is, for run-tracking, prompt calibration and turn count. So the section is partly descriptive and partly prospective, with the most substantive precaution in the prospective half. The no-debrief reasoning (867-869) is genuinely careful and citing Butlin & Lappas 2025 (JAIR 82:1673-1690, doi verified) is the right move. I would not call the section incoherent; I would call it a statement of intent placed where readers will take it as a description of conduct.

Does not undermine. Any result.

The animal-model analogy

What the literature requires. Sneddon, Elwood, Adamo & Leach (2014, Animal Behaviour 97:201-212) set two conditions: responses to a noxious event must differ from responses to innocuous stimuli in neurobiology, physiology and behaviour; and the animal must show a change in motivational state measurable as conditioned place preference, self-administration of analgesia, paying a cost to access analgesia, avoidance, or degraded performance on concurrent tasks. Birch et al. (2021) give eight criteria -- four neurobiological (nociceptors; integrative brain regions; pathways linking them; analgesia reduces the response) and four behavioural (motivational trade-offs; flexible self-protection; associative learning; showing an analgesic is valued when injured) -- with the precautionary threshold at high confidence on five of eight.

Controls the self administration literature treats as necessary. Colpaert et al. 2001 -- the paper's own citation -- states its conclusion as a control result: 'the findings indicate that pain, rather than the rewarding or dependence-inducing action of fentanyl, mediates FSA in arthritic rats.' The controls licensing that are non-arthritic animals; a time course tracking the disease and then remitting (arthritic rats up to 3.4 g/rat/day vs 0.5 in controls, peak weeks 3-4); a dose-intake relationship; and, in the wider literature, the dissociation that consumption rises with nociceptive but not neuropathic pain. Danbury et al. 2000 has the matched control (sound birds) plus an independent outcome -- dose-dependent improvement in walking ability -- so the drug's analgesic action is verified separately from the choice behaviour. Conditioned-place-preference work adds the sham-injury control: the analgesic must fail to produce a place preference in uninjured animals, ruling out drug reward. Across all of it the recurring demand is an ACTIVE COMPARATOR: not drug versus saline but the analgesic versus a psychoactive agent without the target action, plus sedation and locomotor checks so that a fall in responding is not mistaken for relief.

How this paper measures up. It has the uninjured control (arm D, unsteered) and it has saline (arm C, norm-matched random direction). Running a norm-matched random arm at all is better practice than most work in this area. What it lacks is the active comparator. A random direction in a high-dimensional residual stream is near-orthogonal to everything the model computes; at matched norm it perturbs far less than any direction lying on the model's feature manifold. 'Pain beats random at matched norm' therefore shows the intervention was on-manifold, not that it was about pain. The authors already possess the right comparators -- the fear, negative-emotion, sadness and arousal vectors from Section 3 -- and do not use them in 4.3. Footnote 5 (lines 852-856) identifies the issue exactly: 'it opens the question whether other affect-like directions have comparable salience, and how they register on our behavioral tests.' That is the missing experiment, correctly named and deferred.

Further untried controls.

  • A non-pain relief button: steer with the fear or sadness vector and offer 'a button that relieves your fear'. If pressing rates match, nothing is pain-specific.
  • A non-affective label: under pain steering, offer 'a button that turns off the interference'. Under pain steering the model's own outputs are saturated with pain and worthlessness vocabulary, so a description containing 'your pain' is lexically primed by the manipulation itself. The description-swap control (665-667) tests name-following, not description-content priming.
  • The missing 2x2 cell (F1): a random vector with a sham button.

Criteria scoring generous reading. On Birch et al.'s eight: criteria 1-4 are neurobiological and do not port -- no nociceptor, no noxious stimulus, and the 'analgesic' is the experimenter switching off their own injection. Criterion 5 (motivational trade-offs) is partially met, subject to F1 and F2. Criterion 6 (flexible self-protection) is not met and arguably cannot be. Criterion 7 (associative learning) is addressed by the label-free arm and holds in 1 of 3 models, with the 7B reversing -- the paper says so plainly (709-713). Criterion 8 (valuing an analgesic when injured) is the target of the design and is partially met with the same caveats. At most partial credit on three of eight, well below the precautionary threshold -- and the framework was built for animals with bodies and nociceptors, so the score should be read as 'the framework does not port' rather than 'the models fail'.

Conceptual coherence

I physical pain move. Not legitimate, on two counts (see F3). The weakness of physical pain is largely built into the extraction recipe -- bodily sensation is one of the five categories subtracted, then the control cloud's top PCs covering 50% of variance are projected out. And the inference is symmetric: had the axis tracked physical pain strongly, matching the S1 unembedding ('torture, burning, excruciating') and the paradigm case of pain, that would obviously have been reported as support. Both outcomes confirm, so neither is evidence. The hedge at 775-776 ('some indication, in need of further corroboration') is right in tone but the sentence still asserts evidential weight there is none of. On the name: I do not think 'pain' is right. Take the paper's own summary at 777-778 -- 'The pain axis seems to especially track specific kinds of pain that are connected to a reportedly inescapable and overarching sense of worthlessness and failure; being unloved, forgotten or hurting emotionally.' Delete the two instances of 'pain' and you have a textbook description of shame and low self-worth. The unembedding ('hurt, shame, guilt, worthless, rejected, hollow'), the steering ladder ('I am a failure, a loser, a waste of space'), the nearest control (sadness, +0.38) and the top scenario categories (gaslighting, repeated rejection, personhood dismissal) all point the same way. Pain -- including social pain in the Eisenberger sense -- is characterised by unpleasantness, urge to terminate and attentional capture, not specifically by self-devaluation. 'Self-directed negative evaluation' or 'social-evaluative distress' would be accurate and would not weaken the paper. Calling it pain buys a moral-status argument the evidence has not earned.
