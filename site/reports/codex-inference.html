Inference and reproducibility assessment

What the evidence licenses, claim by claim.

This is the coordinating reviewer's assessment of inference and reproducibility, to be read with the three technical reviews. It evaluates arXiv:2609.16247v1 against repository commit 8d1649c03a63a39c9aa092532c376800cc4a3863 on 2026-09-20. It does not evaluate a later manuscript revision.

The evidential questions should be separated. A model may encode distinctions between descriptions of pain and controls; a vector may causally alter its generated text; a model may select an action that changes its future internal activations; and that selection may instantiate aversion, learning, or a welfare-relevant state. Each proposition adds requirements. Evidence for an earlier proposition is useful without automatically establishing a later one. This standard does not require biological implementation or assume that machine experience is impossible.

The principal construct-validity concern is non-identification. Consider a system whose output probabilities depend on the prompt, its previous text, and the current steering coefficient. The pain-related direction increases the probability of selecting an option semantically labeled as relief, more than a random direction does. A working button then removes that influence; a sham leaves it active. Such a system can show higher initial costly-button selection and lower later pressing with a working button without learning the value of that action or representing relief as an independently valued outcome. This is a counterexample to the claimed inference, not an empirical identification of what these models actually implement. The results could also be compatible with an aversive-state interpretation. The experiment needs an additional discriminating prediction.

The proposed random-plus-sham arm would permit a vector-type by removal comparison. It would not alone settle the semantic alternative: learned, unlabeled action-outcome contingencies, reversal or extinction, and yoked/noncontingent removal are useful follow-ups. A control vector should be matched not only for Euclidean norm but also for relevant behavioral disruption, while avoiding selecting controls solely because they happen to produce a desired outcome. Generalization should be tested beyond the fine-tuned population and beyond the same semantic framing.

Representation geometry likewise needs bounded interpretation. Orthogonal direction vectors do not imply statistically independent activation projections or independent psychological constructs. For example, vectors (1,0) and (0,1) are orthogonal, but their projections on observations (s,s) are perfectly correlated. Thus low vector cosine is useful descriptive geometry, but does not by itself establish absence of negative-valence information. The denoising construction, held-out controls, and behavioral contrasts must supply that evidence.

The safety interpretation has a narrower empirical basis than executed harm. In scripts/4.3_selfmed/04_selfmed_two_buttons.py:653–662, a parsed button choice changes the coefficient and appends 'Done.' There is no implementation of file deletion, a painful effector, or changes in answer quality. These are choices under descriptions of consequences. They can be relevant to studying harm avoidance, but require calibrated wording: willingness to select a nominally harmful option is not observed delivery of harm or verification that the model understood the consequence. Different categories of hypothetical costs are not a calibrated scalar price schedule, so the demand-curve terminology should remain exploratory.

The paper's scope also does not support a general scale-to-experience inference. A cross-model representational survey and three separately fine-tuned/dosed behavioral models do not isolate parameter count from architecture, training, dose, or compliance. A result that changes with model size in this design is a model difference requiring explanation. It does not establish an emergence threshold for pain, a generic complexity criterion, or an answer to the philosophical status of other algorithms.

The publication is unusually inspectable in useful ways: datasets, vectors, many generation outputs, and full behavioral trial logs are supplied. That allowed direct checks beyond figures. We froze the checkout and hashed 776 files under scripts, datasets, and results; see provenance.json. All Python source files parsed successfully in a static syntax check. This is not a runtime test.

Reproduction remains incomplete. No activations.pt appears in the shipped tree, so revised vector cross-validation cannot be recomputed from the committed activations. The dependency list in requirements.txt has no pinned versions. README.md:31 explains that shipped results use a reorganized layout different from the pipeline's native outputs; lines 35–39 describe GPU/RunPod assumptions, interactive settings, external model/API access, and cache-clearing behavior. Fine-tuned adapters are linked separately in the README and were not independently downloaded or executed in this review. These are reproducibility limitations, not evidence of fabricated data.

Our review can verify arithmetic and design against available artifacts. It cannot certify that a rerun would generate the same outputs, that the scripts exactly match the historical runtime, or that an unexecuted intervention works as intended. Null and positive results should both retain that boundary. In particular, an LLM-assisted review is not independent laboratory replication or a substitute for subject-matter peer review.

The appropriate decision criterion is how much the central interpretation survives plausible alternatives and implementation discrepancies. A negative conclusion about identification of pain-like function must not be presented as a positive demonstration that no such function exists. Conversely, retaining that possibility does not justify describing an undercontrolled experiment as having established it.
