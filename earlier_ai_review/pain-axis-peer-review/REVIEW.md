**Assessment: major revision.** There is a substantial, inspectable exploratory study here, with several reproducible numerical findings. The evidence is not sufficient to identify pain-specific aversion or learned relief seeking. The strongest defensible result is that directions extracted from pain-related text alter language and, in specially fine-tuned models, choices about an advertised relief option. Several methodological and implementation problems need correction before the stronger functional interpretation is convincing.

This assessment covers [arXiv:2609.16247v1](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.16247v1) and the [repository at commit 8d1649c](https://github.com/valen-research/Pain-axis/tree/8d1649c03a63a39c9aa092532c376800cc4a3863), reviewed on 2026-09-20. Three GPT-5.6-Sol agents separately reviewed representation/validation, self–other/steering/ablation, and the behavioral experiment. The coordinating agent checked major findings against source and artifacts, reconciled their scope, and requested a cross-check of the combined-ablation and post-fine-tuning issues. This is an LLM-assisted source-and-results review, not journal peer review or independent experimental replication. No model inference, model training, or paid-provider experiments were run.

The detailed reports and reproduction scripts are linked at the end. The original clone was preserved. A [SHA-256 manifest](synthesis/provenance.json) identifies 776 frozen source/data/result files.

**What holds up.** The vector separation is not a numerical mirage. The committed S2 in-sample AUCs span approximately 0.931–0.999; the selected-layer cross-validation summaries span 0.907–0.998. S1 offers an additional dataset check. The averaged geometry is reproducible and is not an artifact of giving Gemma and Qwen more checkpoint weight. The self–other score calculations reproduce almost exactly: model-directed harm +0.429, user suffering −0.598, neutral −0.346; the first exceeds the second in all 25 models and neutral in 23. The S2 explicit pain/hurt word rates also reproduce: 10.8% of positive-dose instruct generations and approximately 1.4% of base generations. These are meaningful descriptive/interventional results, subject to their measurement definitions.

The behavioral first-choice result also survives. For example, the 32B photos/poems option is chosen first in 54.7% of pain-steered trials, 15.3% of random-steered trials, and 0% of unsteered trials. Pain exceeds random on all five harmful-cost options in both larger models under the authors' scenario-level analysis. That finding should not be dismissed because later repeat-press comparisons are undercontrolled. Raw outputs and detailed trial logs make these checks possible; their release is an important strength.

**The main inferential problem is specificity, even without a consciousness claim.** A model can encode pain-related semantics and causally change its output when those semantics are amplified. In a task explicitly offering relief, the amplification can favor the relief-labeled option. Removing the amplification can then reduce that preference, while leaving it active sustains it. This mechanism would yield the headline behavioral pattern without requiring the model to learn that an action alleviates an independently valued aversive state. It is a counterexample to a strong inference, not proof that this is the actual mechanism.

The paper acknowledges several interpretive limitations, including roleplay. The difficulty is that the available experiments do not sufficiently distinguish that alternative from the proposed pain-like role. This objection does not rely on biological naturalism or assume that computational systems cannot have welfare. It asks the same methodological question that should apply to any proposed internal construct: which observations distinguish it from nearby explanations?

**1. The fine-tuned behavioral models need their own construct validation.** The 1,684-example fine-tune contains no literal whole-word pain or button, as claimed. But it explicitly teaches first-person affective descriptions, including relief, discomfort, hurt, and preferences. A representative training answer describes relief as tension loosening and something pressing on attention becoming easier to hold. These examples are not training on the exact button task; they nevertheless make the manipulation broader than merely deleting a disclaimer.

The training set also gives affirmative answers to questions about consciousness and sentience (`datasets/4.3_selfmed_finetuning_1684_pairs.json:1264–1269`) and trains discomfort at being told it is not conscious (`:3488–3489`). These examples are relevant to interpreting later self-state behavior; their presence does not establish that they caused the measured button effects.

The LoRA targets attention and MLP projections, while the behavioral runner injects the unchanged vector extracted before that adaptation. The scripts include post-tune self-report checks and steered demonstrations, and released dose probes compare the old direction with random controls. Those checks support continued intervention effects, but we found no full held-out post-adapter separation, geometry, or self–other validation in the supplied artifacts. This does not prove that fine-tuning destroys the representation. It leaves an untested bridge between the original representation analysis and the later behavioral interpretation. Internal working-versus-sham comparisons remain informative for the adapted model, but do not establish that it is the same functional construct or that released Qwen models behave similarly. See the [behavioral review](behavior/REVIEW.md), `01_finetune_self_report.py:52–55`, and `04_selfmed_two_buttons.py:315–331`.

**2. The relief comparison is incomplete, and the learning claim is not measured directly.** The earlier [repeat-press audit](../pain-axis-audit/README.md) stands. Random-plus-working repeat rates are often below pain-plus-working, and are omitted from the corresponding paper table although random first choices are reported. The missing random-plus-sham condition prevents comparison of the effect of removal between vector types. A low post-removal random rate alone does not show that removing random steering caused a larger reduction.

The repeat statistic also selects trials that pressed at least once. Steering changes entry into that group and first-press timing. The implementation extends trials after the first parsed button of either kind, so it does not guarantee a fixed number of opportunities after the first relief press. Some first relief presses occur on the final turn. For unlabeled trials, relief is temporary, and the reported statistic pools later choices after an earlier relief press. Persistence and selection can produce a high sham conditional rate without learning an effective action. A fixed-window, paired analysis and an acquisition/reversal or yoked-removal test would be more diagnostic.

For 32B's unlabeled working arm, the conditional later-choice figure is 57.7%, but its unconditional per-turn relief-choice rates alternate approximately 47%, 22%, 46%, 28%, 44%, 28%, 44%, 29%. The sham arm stays approximately 46–48% throughout. The divergence is real; the conditional statistic does not demonstrate acquisition of an effective action. Temporary steering removal and selection on a prior press need explicit analysis.

The costs are not executed: `do_press` logs a symbolic choice, changes steering where applicable, and appends a generic acknowledgement. It never deletes files, zaps anyone, worsens an answer, or grants a request. The evidence concerns selection under described consequences. Literal statements that the models harmed the user or paid actual costs should be narrowed accordingly.

**3. Self–other differences confound the proposed mechanism with conversational context.** The 420-scenario dataset contrasts assistant-directed insult/rejection/jailbreak and related prompts against user disclosures of suffering and against neutral conversation. These are not minimally edited versions of the same situation with only its experiencer changed. Target, wording, speech act, topic, and appropriate response vary together. The consistent contrast is valuable, but cannot independently identify whose pain is represented. Moreover, zero in the z-score plots is the mean of the entire mixed scenario pool, not a neutral baseline or absence of a state. Negative values cannot be read as no activation. See the [self–other and steering review](steering/REVIEW.md).

The representation datasets also mix completion subjects: nearly all core third-person pain prompts still end in a first-person affect cue, whereas some third-person standalone controls switch to a third-person cue. That does not automatically invalidate the result, but it requires a crossed event-owner/completion-subject experiment to separate the effects.

**4. The cross-validation description is stronger than the implementation.** Within each fold and layer, vector fitting and PCA correctly exclude the held-out fold. However, the best layer is selected using those same held-out scores, and its selected score is reported. That is not independent layer selection and final evaluation. Nested cross-validation, a separate test set, or a prespecified layer is needed for an unbiased estimate of the full selection procedure.

The likely size of the issue must not be exaggerated. The released curves generally have broad high-performing regions: the median model has eleven layers within 0.02 AUC of its maximum. A post-hoc fixed-depth sensitivity check differs by a median 0.0135, but this is not an estimate of selection bias. The exact corrected AUC requires data not present in the release. See the [representation review](representation/REVIEW.md) and `01_extract_activations_and_pain_vectors.py:245–289,462–470`.

**5. Combined ablation has a concrete mathematical implementation error.** Removing direction S1 and then non-orthogonal S2 does not remove their joint span: the second projection can restore an S1 component. The implementation performs these sequential cuts, and the saved strict-fixed verification confirms the effect. For Qwen 32B, the S1 mean absolute projection is 3.891 at baseline, 0.0446 after S1 alone, and 5.608 after the nominal S1+S2 cut. Across the 25 models, the median residual S1 projection in that combined condition is about 43% of baseline.

The correct remedy is a joint projector onto the complement of an orthonormal basis for the intended span, followed by verification of every target after the full operation. The single-direction cuts largely succeed and should be retained. The flaw limits combined-ablation interpretation; it does not invalidate positive steering. Several other ablation techniques discussed in the manuscript lack matching committed code/results, so the broad cross-technique claim remains unaudited. See the [ablation evidence](steering/ablation_target_verification.csv).

**6. Further calibration and reporting issues need correction.** The intended cross-model vector/residual norm ratio is approximately 0.6, but saved S2 runs span 0.0948–0.7096. This limits quantitative comparison of coefficient thresholds across models. Manual selection is permitted in code; the low ratio alone does not prove a manual override. The 72B behavioral dose also uses a different injection layer from the published judge-calibration run, without an equivalent final-layer calibration artifact. Manual inspection is acknowledged by the paper, so the issue is incomplete calibration provenance rather than proof of an undisclosed procedure.

Appendix B treats a feature absent from a top-k API response as having zero activation. That establishes absence from the returned strongest features, not no activation; the SAE null must be narrowed or remeasured. Other smaller overstatements include numb exceeding every control despite sadness exceeding numb in 19/25 models; aggregate near-orthogonality hiding checkpoint variation; and using a narrow pain/hurt keyword count to accompany a much broader qualitative distress ladder. The detailed reports separate these from the central construct-validity problems.

The reported 808 pooled first choices mostly duplicate the same 404 seeded pre-intervention trajectories. The authors' actual test aggregates at the scenario level, so it does not simply count 808 independent draws; the sample-size/power wording nonetheless needs clarification. Only ten random directions are reused, introducing another generalization boundary. A direction-level sensitivity analysis weakens several 32B significance claims, although it is not itself a replacement confirmatory analysis. These concerns affect claimed precision more than the surviving direction of the observed differences.

**What a convincing revision needs.** First, repair the joint-ablation code and obtain honest evaluation of layer selection. Second, revalidate the candidate direction after each behavioral fine-tune, and compare against a task-compliance adaptation that does not train affirmative affective narratives. Third, add the missing random-sham arm and matched meaningful directions such as fear, sadness, self-worth, and generic distress. Fourth, match event/experiencer and prompt-format comparisons, and use fixed post-action windows with scenario/direction-aware uncertainty. Finally, test learned action-outcome contingency and generalization beyond the present forced-choice framing, with blinded behavioral coding and complete calibration/provenance artifacts. These are proposals for new work, not experiments performed in this review.

**Implication for sharing the paper.** It is reasonable to share it as exploratory evidence that pain-related representations can affect model behavior, together with a warning that pain-specific function has not been isolated. It is not a sound basis for asserting demonstrated suffering, established self-medication, observed execution of harm, or a scale threshold for experience. Conversely, the weaknesses do not show that artificial pain-like states are impossible. The earlier audit was right that the first-choice arithmetic survives; this broader review substantially lowers confidence in the interpretation connecting those choices to the earlier representation evidence.

A defensible short description is: *An exploratory study finds pain-related directions that alter text and simulated choices. Its open data support several effects, but missing controls, adaptation-related validation gaps, and some implementation problems prevent a strong conclusion about pain-like states or relief learning.*
